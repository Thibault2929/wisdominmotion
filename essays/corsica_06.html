Day five

Two hikers passed as I woke up. It was not even six o'clock in the morning, but not wanting to be spotted by too many people, I decided to pack my belongings as quickly as possible. The trail followed the river along the valley until it turned right towards the Bergerie de l'Inzecche, then left again towards the Lake of Ninu. Only the bark of a dog pierced the silence, and I could make out the two young walkers I had encountered while still half asleep. Like the previous valley, the area around the lake was covered in pozzines and enclosed by gentle slopes, giving it the characteristic U-shape carved by glaciers, since even Corsica's mountains were buried under massive ice sheets during the ice ages. After passing the lake, I pressed on west, leaving the valley behind as I climbed towards the Bocca à Réta. The area was sparsely vegetated, with only a few trees poking up here and there. Following the mountain flank to the Col di San-Pietru, the path descended north, and before long I found myself in dense pine forest once more. I reached the hotel Castellu di Vergio located along the D84 road around 9 am, just in time for breakfast. While ordering a cappuccino and a pain au chocolat, I was relieved to see a small shop selling sports gear. They also had socks, which I badly needed, as I had forgotten to pack two extra pairs. My simple short white socks were now worn-out, crusty and brown. I did not hesitate to buy a pair of hiking socks proudly labelled "Made in France" that cost me 26 euros and are my sole souvenir from the GR 20. My old socks in tatters, I tossed them in the nearest bin before enjoying my early lunch.
